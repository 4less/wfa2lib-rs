flexalign vs protal and minibwa on protal-mini

flexalign main@204aac9 against protal v0.6.0 and minibwa 0.7-r421. All three indexes were built from the same protal-mini marker reference (372,999 marker genes of 3,600 species, 429 MB). Same reads (996,424 pairs; two thirds from species in the reference, but mostly from genome regions outside the marker genes), 16 threads, warm page cache, scored by the benchmark's score.py at species level. What the precision fix changed

flexalign 204aac9 (now main) differs from 55dca8f in two ways. --min-ani defaults to 0.95, the species boundary, instead of 0.85: most alignments between 85% and 95% identity were reads of species the reference lacks, placed on a relative's marker (wrong mates 15,823 → 10,842, correct 60,797 → 60,788). And a mate whose partner did not align to the same reference loses 25 MAPQ, staying at 1 or above: such mates were 63–83% right at MAPQ 20–39 against 93–96% for paired mates, so a MAPQ cutoff used to drop good pairs before bad lone mates. Speed is unchanged; bacteroides loses 248 of 1.95 M correct mates (−0.013 pts).

The simulated reads come from the reference genomes themselves (correct mates average 0.27 edits). Reads from strains 95–97% away from the reference would lose some sensitivity at the 0.95 floor; this benchmark cannot measure how much.

Setup

  • flexalign ran with --index-format hashed --index-mmap; wall time includes the index load. Median of 5 runs.
  • protal ran alignment only (--no_profile --no_strains), so its taxonomic profiling step is not part of the time. Index: protal --build --reference reference.fna, single-threaded as protal requires. Median of 3 runs.
  • minibwa ran as the benchmark runs it, minibwa map -x sr; index minibwa index -l -b 1g. Median of 3 runs.
  • Truth labels every read with its source species. 1,319,106 of the 1,992,848 mates (66%) come from one of the reference's species, but most fall outside the marker genes, and the truth does not say which overlap a marker. So recall is measured against all mates, as score.py does, and its ceiling is well below 100%. A mate counts once, by its primary alignment.
  • protal's build reads the FASTA only from --reference; without it, it writes an all-zero index and reports no error. It also needs a reference.map, generated here with the same byte-offset rule as the full r226 database.
  • Machine: AMD Ryzen AI 9 HX 370, 24 threads, Linux 7.2.
